# Սմբատ Սպարապետ, Դատաստանագիրք: notes

Working notes for the modern Eastern Armenian
translation. Not on the shelf and not on the Pages site.

Source: Digilib book 332,
<https://www.digilib.am/book/332/>, one part:
`books/grabar/smbat-sparapet/1-datastanagirk/01.txt`;
the translation is at the same path under
`books/ashkharhabar/`. Line numbers are the same in
both.

## What the book is

The law book of the Armenian kingdom of Cilicia,
made by Smbat the Constable (brother of King Hetum I,
son of Constantine of Baberon) in the year 714 of the
Armenian era (1265). He says in the preface that he
"moved it from old, hard and incomprehensible books
into our easy-to-hear, everyday words", from the
Grabar of the older law books into the Middle
Armenian of his own time (with French, Greek, Arabic
and Persian words: `պրինս`, `նոմոս`, `պռոյգ`,
`պռիստիմոն`, `հալալ`, `հարամ`, `ալան`).

The text has `[Ա]`...`[ՄԲ]` sections (the numbers
run `Ա`–`Ղ`, `Ճ`–`ՃՂԹ`, `Մ`–`ՄԲ`; line 1628 has
`[ՂՃԵ]` for `[ՃՂԵ]`, copied as it stands) and these
title lines: `Առաջաբանութիւն` (line 5),
`Իրաւունք Վաճառականաց`, `Իրաւունք Ծնաւղաց եւ Որդոց`,
`Վասն Գրաւեոյ...`, `Ժառանգութիւն Հայրենեաց`,
`Իրաւունք Ծառութեան`, `Վասն Վնասաց...`,
`Վասն Տիաթկերոյ`.

## Part 1: what each range covers

(Lines of the sentence-split Grabar; the ranges follow
the chunks of the translation.)

- 1–117: preface (Psalm 118, Smbat's colophon);
  `[Ա]`: succession to the throne, a king's rights
  (coin, castles, bridges), war and spoils, taxes of
  the peasants, craftsmen and doctors.
- 119–324: `[Բ]`–`[ԼԳ]`: sons of condemned princes,
  mines and finds in the ground, water and game,
  fights, labourers; the church: priests and
  bishops, bribery, vardapets, archbishops and the
  catholicos, the shares of priests, monks (after
  St Sahak).
- 326–533: more of Sahak's rules; monasteries and
  hostels, feasts; spouses taken captive; priests'
  property, exemption from tax; offices of the church.
- 535–737: the nine orders of the church and the
  orders of the Byzantine court (`[ԿԳ]`); building
  churches; monks; blasphemy; teachers and students.
- 739–950: trees, animals and stones that kill; false
  priests and beggars; church property; kings and
  princes (who may judge and exile whom); marriage,
  divorce, madness and illness in marriage; killing
  for adultery.
- 952–1163: more marriage law (second marriages,
  captivity, age, the unfit); inheritance of land,
  parents and children, suicides; pledges.
- 1165–1375: trade (houses, animals, bees, jars,
  gardens, mills, fraud, prices); wills; slaves;
  damage by animals, fire and floods; killing by
  children at play.
- 1377–1594: killing and wounding (willing and
  unwilling, drunkenness, medicine, accidents);
  craftsmen and hired labour; borders; new villages;
  deposits and loans; usury.
- 1596–1707: canon and court; false witnesses; lost
  things; hired animals and drivers; possession by
  demons; pastures and walls; mills; wages.

## How it was done

By `TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md`, 2026-11-15, in sixteen
chunks of 100–112 lines, each checked against the
Grabar (section 6 of the guide) before the next, and
the whole checked at the end: 1707 lines, 203
contents entries in both files, all blank lines and
all locked `[Ա]` lines the same.

Decisions:

- the title line stays «Սմբատ Սպարապետ,
  Դատաստանագիրք · 1/1»; the second line (title in
  title case, not capitals) is translated;
- `[Ա]` lines are copied exactly; the lines that
  title a group (`Իրաւունք Վաճառականաց`) are
  translated (`Վաճառականների Իրավունքը`);
- the Grabar has no «», only a few parentheses: none
  added; the stray « in line 144 is not copied;
- terms kept: `դեկան` (a coin, also `դեկան հատել`
  for minting), `պերպեռատ` (hyperpyron), `մթխալ`,
  `դանկ` (1 mithqal = 6 dank, line 1089), `պռիստիմոն`
  (Greek *prostimon*, a pledge or fine), `պառիկոս`
  (a serf, Greek *paroikos*), `պրինս`, `մարգիզ`,
  `նոմիկոս` (notary), `ջանուն` (a canon), `փիրիսկոպոս`
  (a periodeutes), `հոլանի`, `ոնոփորոն`, `նափորտ`,
  `ուրար`, `շուրջառ`, `սքեմ` (clothing of the
  orders), the ranks of the court (`կանդիտոր`,
  `վեստիտոր`, `ստրատորոս`...);
- `եւ` → `և`, `Եվ` at a sentence start; the
  Grabar's `.` and `:`-ending sentences end in `։`;
- legal wording for the punishments is kept as the
  text has it, with no softening (cutting off the
  hand, the nose, the genitals: `սեռական անդամ`).

## Notes on the text (guesses and doubts)

Words understood from the sense and written in
Armenian letters as they stand, or only guessed:

- `պոման` is `պայման` (a condition), lines 39, 694,
  816, 875, 1171...; `կահրա` is Arabic *qahr*,
  force (334 "strictly", 918 "by force", 1318,
  1492); `վզեն` is a loss or damage (509, 796, 1155,
  1159); `ալան` is "in the open" (82, 557);
  `կեցցնել` is "to prove" (198, 806, 961, 973);
  `նեհ` is price (Persian *narkh*); `խաստ` is
  damage; `հարամ` and `հալալ` as in Arabic;
  `ցուլ` (a bull) is the lover of an adulteress
  (901–921); `ձետ`/`ձուք` are the penis and
  testicles (918–929); `քշտան` and `հարուն` are
  animals that kick and that butt (1184, 1233); `բիր`
  (1417, 1528) a cudgel (guessed from `բրով`).
- Not found, kept as they are in Armenian letters:
  `թըտպրով`, `բահրով` (240); `զրհ` (87); `ածուք`
  (97); `տարպա` (102); `ընձել` (133); `լաւղաւռիտս`
  (336); `սալար` (337); `պրոպատիկե` (331, maybe the
  Probatic pool); `քթեթ` (381); `խասապ`, `շողհաց`
  (388–389); `զիտառ` (556); `քուրձիկ` (501); `քուշ`
  (623); `երին ու երբուծ` (510, 724); `գղթոր` and
  `մահլապ` (131, plants or gums); `պրիսմեն` (89);
  `մուրզ` (739); `մատնեհարեն` (745); `ծառութրար`
  (753); `զետառ` (759); `լիճ ճորտ` (778, perhaps
  *liege*); `մոլոզոն` (667, 787); `գոդ`, `պիսակ`
  (815, 981, 1043: kinds of skin disease);
  `առուգնայք` (991); `աղչնաւք`, `խլինք` (996);
  `ընջուղ` (1337); `կոթրու` (1205); `կոճակ`
  (1207); `դուք`, `մուխտասիբ` (1239); `հեճկով`
  (1450); `բահրակ` (1456, 1464, 1502, 1630, 1633);
  `ցզբեղ` (1435, 1576); `զիտա` (1496); `պզման`
  (1516); `ագին` (1661); `պախրե`, `գեհ` (1652);
  `կապճկեր` (1682); `խսում` (950); the court ranks
  and clothing at 640–667 (`եղենտիար`, `վզնատաշենք`,
  `կմբրաւոր`, `ապուկալ`, `գրատ`, `ուրած վարոց`...).
- Guessed meanings: `մռտէ` (203, 227) "turns
  against"; `հաւնովրեն` (227, 430) "punish"/"the one
  who agrees"; `ռոճկավոր` for `հռոքվորե` (144);
  `յիստկի` (450); `հիլայած` (258) "accused";
  `աւժիթք` (1394) "rewards"; `մուխատրա` "danger"
  (1460, 1490); `փրթման` "free" (972, 1551);
  `քաշնաց` (936) "suffering"; `ինչուի`
  "until"; `այրել` is sometimes "wholly" (57, 1502)
  and sometimes "to burn" (484, 486).
- Lines 74–89: the sense of the spoils
  (`շահ`, `ծեծ` = a raid, `յիշաւ` = a campaign) is
  guessed from the words; the shares and the term
  `ի ճանպահ` need a second reading.
- Line 1030 breaks off (`...քաւէլ թէ չլինի եկեղեցոյ
  կամ։`) and line 1693 ends with `...`; both as they
  are in the source. Line 1134 (`երկու երկնաքարերի`)
  and line 1139 (`հաւնովորումն`) are unclear.
- The reader is to check the places above first when
  reviewing; a person who knows 13th-century Cilician
  Armenian or the Assizes of Antioch (the code is known
  to draw on them) should be able to settle most of
  them.
