# How did our ancestors understand Armenian history?

theme: books

Today we often read history as an account of kings, wars, cities and peoples. But for the medieval Armenian historians it meant much more.

They described the events of their own time as a continuation of the story that begins in the Bible.

For them the spread of Christianity in Armenia was not simply the adoption of a new faith. It was a continuation of the apostolic preaching, the struggle of light against darkness, a witness to God's presence in the Armenian land.

That is why their works so often contain quotations from the Bible, theological reflections and comparisons with the events of the Gospels.

In many historical works published in the Soviet years, the religious content was often pushed into the background. Sometimes it was cut, or presented only as a cultural feature of the period.

As a result, we sometimes read the events without understanding what meaning the authors themselves gave them.

When we return to the original texts, we discover another side of Armenian history. We see not only what happened to our people, but also how our ancestors understood their place in the history of the Christian world.

To truly understand history, we sometimes have to return to the language and the way of thinking in which it was written.
