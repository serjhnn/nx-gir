# History of Taron

written: attributed to the 4th and 7th centuries, put together between the 7th and 10th
kind: a history of Taron and the Glak monastery
source: Wikipedia · the book's colophon

The history of Taron and of the monastery of Surb Karapet (Glak), from the days of Gregory the Illuminator, when the old temples there were overthrown, to the wars of the Mamikonians against the Persians in the 6th and 7th centuries. In the colophon Hovhan Mamikonean writes that Zenob began the book and that the fathers of the monastery after him each wrote of their own time; he himself translated 28 chapters, added 10 and brought them together as 38.

Sections [Ա] to [Դ] are attributed to Zenob: Gregory's letter to Caesarea and its answer, which Zenob gives in his account, then Zenob's letter to the Syrian bishops about the place of Innaknean and the first battle fought at Ardzan, and the second battle. Sections [Ե] to [ԺԱ] are Hovhan Mamikonean's: the abbots of Glak after Zenob and the wars of Mushegh, the campaigns of Mihran, Vakhtang, Tigran and Varduhr, the story of the cross of Tsitsarn, the death of Tiran, and the author's colophon.
