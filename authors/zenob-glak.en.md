# Zenob Glak

years: 4th century
role: abbot, historian
source: Wikipedia · Armenian Wikipedia · Russian Wikipedia

By tradition a disciple of Gregory the Illuminator, who came with him from Caesarea; some sources call him a Syrian (Zenob the Syrian). He was the first abbot of the monastery of Surb Karapet in Taron and the bishop of the house of the Mamikonians, and the monastery was called Glakavank after him.

The first part of the History of Taron is attributed to him: at Gregory's command he writes to the Syrian bishops how the new faith took hold in Taron. Hovhan Mamikonean continued the book. Scholars doubt that this part is really his and date the whole book to the 7th to 10th centuries; the first to mention Zenob's history is Ukhtanes (10th century).
