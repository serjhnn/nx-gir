# History of the Armenians

written: probably the 470s
kind: a history of 4th-century Armenia
source: Wikipedia · the Digilib edition

Known as the Epic Histories (Buzandaran Patmutiwnk), a name that means "tales of the bards". It tells of Armenia from the death of Gregory the Illuminator in 331 to the partition of the country between Persia and Rome in 387, in three strands woven together: the last Arsacid kings, the patriarchs of the house of Gregory, and the Mamikonian commanders, the faithful defenders of unworthy kings. The author drew on the tales and songs of the bards (gusans) of his own time.

The surviving books are numbered three to six, which has led scholars to ask whether the first two were lost. The earliest full manuscript is of 1599, and the work was first printed in Constantinople in 1730. Here it is in Digilib's text, 148 sections in three parts.
