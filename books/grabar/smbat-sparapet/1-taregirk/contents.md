# Contents

## Smbat Sparapet — *Տարեգիրք* (Chronicle)

This contents guide describes the fifty-five Grabar text files currently imported into this directory. Several files are explicitly marked as indexed excerpts whose completeness has not been verified. The numbered entries follow the Digilib section identifiers; they are not consecutive chapter numbers. Descriptions are brief reader-oriented summaries, not translations. They avoid reproducing graphic passages.

1. **Section 410 — The Armenian coronation of Gagik.** The entry records Gagik's formal anointing as king in Ani and then turns to a change of ruler in the Byzantine Empire.
2. **Section 418 — Byzantine imperial succession.** A chronicle entry focused on the Byzantine court and the succession of emperors.
3. **Section 421 — Armenia and the neighbouring powers.** Political and military developments involving Armenian rulers and the Byzantine-Arab frontier.
4. **Section 424 — Armenian rule and captivity.** Events surrounding an Armenian ruler, regional power struggles, and captivity.
5. **Section 432 — A new Catholicos and the Georgian-Armenian alliance.** The succession of Catholicos Vahan by Step'annos, followed by a major threat to the region and the cooperation of Armenian and Georgian forces.
6. **Section 494 — Earthquake and political upheaval.** A devastating earthquake in 1045 is followed by accounts of conflicts involving Byzantine, Persian, and regional powers.
7. **Section 508 — Sebastia and the Armenian princes.** The chronicle describes the crisis around Sebastia and the position of Armenian rulers under Byzantine authority.
8. **Section 511 — Raids, compassion, and regional response.** Raids in Armenian districts are recounted alongside the efforts of a local ruler to secure the release of captives.
9. **Section 523 — Gregory's travels and the Armenian Church abroad.** Gregory's journey through Constantinople, Rome, and Egypt, his contacts with Christian communities, and developments in the Armenian Church.
10. **Section 541 — Epidemic, a holy relic, and a change of power.** An epidemic and a dispute over a revered relic appear alongside political developments following the death of Malik-Shah.
11. **Section 546 — The First Crusade and Antioch.** Events connected with the First Crusade and the changing political landscape around Antioch.
12. **Section 547 — The Crusader states and their neighbours.** Further accounts of the Crusader principalities and the political relationships among powers in the region.
13. **Section 562 — Edessa and the displacement of its people.** The entry recounts a crisis in Edessa, the departure and eventual return of its inhabitants, and wider events involving the Crusader rulers.
14. **Section 598 — Cilician Armenia and the changing balance of power.** Events around Marash and Tilbashar, including the capture and negotiated release of an Armenian prince's family.
15. **Section 610 — Ani and Georgian-Armenian regional politics.** Campaigns involving the Georgian king Giorgi and the city of Ani, alongside developments concerning Antioch and neighbouring rulers.
16. **Section 616 — The succession of Catholicos Nerses.** The long service of Catholicos Grigoris, the election of Nerses, and the latter's reputation for learning and sacred poetry.
17. **Section 618 — Mleh's rule in Cilicia.** A strongly critical account of Mleh's seizure of power and oppressive conduct in Cilicia. The source includes passages that are not reproduced in this description.
18. **Section 622 — Gregory Tgha becomes Catholicos.** The death of Catholicos Nerses and the appointment of Gregory Tgha, with attention to his learning and the embellishment of the cathedral.
19. **Section 625 — A treaty between the Seljuk sultan and the Byzantine emperor.** Kilij Arslan II confronts the Byzantine emperor near Melitene, then releases him after an agreement is reached.
20. **Section 626 — The death of Manuel I Komnenos.** The death of the Byzantine emperor Manuel I and the accession of his son Alexios.
21. **Section 717 — Hethum, Abaqa Khan, and Antioch.** A diplomatic mission involving Hethum's court is followed by the events leading to the fall of Antioch in 1268.
22. **Section 702 — Hethum's journey to Möngke Khan.** Hethum's journey east, his reception by Möngke, the gifts exchanged, and a dynastic marriage linking the Armenian and Antiochene courts. This entry was captured from Digilib's indexed text; completeness remains unverified.
23. **Section 718 — Earthquake and diplomacy in Cilicia.** A destructive earthquake in Cilicia is followed by Prince Leon's visit to Abaqa Khan and several deaths in the ruling family.


24. **Section 564 — A strange fire in Amid and regional alliances.** The entry records an unusual event in Amid and a coalition of regional rulers during conflict near Antioch. The imported text is only an indexed excerpt and ends before the source entry is complete.
25. **Section 715 — The Mamluk campaign and Hethum's diplomacy.** The passage describes the sultan's campaign, territorial demands, and Hethum's diplomatic efforts. The imported text is only an indexed excerpt and ends mid-entry.

26. **Section 545 — Melitene and the arrival of western nobles.** The entry recounts a threat to Melitene and the arrival of western nobles to support the region. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
27. **Section 555 — Gregory the Witness-Lover and the Armenian Catholicosate.** The passage concerns the reception of Catholicos Barsegh, the care of young members of the Pahlavuni family, and the death and burial of Gregory.
28. **Section 560 — Regional conflict around Tilkurna and Tilbashar.** The entry follows shifting alliances and a confrontation that ends without a battle. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
29. **Section 567 — Baldwin's succession in Jerusalem and the Armenian prince Leon.** The chronicle records a royal succession, developments in Persia, and Leon's growing prominence. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
30. **Section 568 — The death of Roger of Antioch and changes in the region.** The entry records a major defeat, the death of Roger, and subsequent political changes, as well as the Byzantine succession. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
31. **Section 589 — The Catholicosate finds a home at Hromkla.** The account describes displacement in the Taurus region and how Gregory and Nerses secured Hromkla as the Armenian Catholicosate's seat. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
32. **Section 594 — The return of Thoros to his ancestral lands.** Thoros escapes captivity in Constantinople, gathers support, and gradually re-establishes control in the Taurus region. The page was opened directly.
33. **Section 599 — Hromkla becomes the Armenian Catholicosate's seat.** Gregory and Nerses seek a secure home for the Catholicosate; Joscelin grants Hromkla and confirms its use in a written document. The page was opened directly.
34. **Section 619 — Earthquake and conflict in Cilicia.** The entry records an earthquake affecting Antioch and Aleppo and renewed tension between the Roupenian and Het'umid houses. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
35. **Section 630 — Ruben's marriage and Leon's departure.** The chronicle describes Ruben's marriage and Leon's departure for Constantinople after political suspicion arose. The available text is an indexed excerpt.

## Editorial note

This is a partial collection of source sections, not a complete transcription of the Chronicle. The entries above are navigational summaries based on the imported text. Sections 500, 545, 555, 560, 567, 568, 589, 619, 630, 637, 647, 655, 656–658, 660, 668, 669, and 708 were captured from indexed text and require comparison with the original pages or scans. Sections 564 and 715 are explicitly partial excerpts. Sections 594 and 599 were opened directly; the other imported sections still require source comparison to confirm completeness. Section 708 intentionally contains only the non-operational political and ecclesiastical portion returned by the index; the omitted continuation has not been reconstructed. Sections 656–658 remain combined because the indexed result did not make their boundaries clear. The source text remains in Grabar; no translation or editorial correction is implied.

**Source:** [Digilib — Տարեգիրք, attributed to Սմբատ Սպարապետ](https://www.digilib.am/book/333/)


36. **Section 500 — Sebastia and the accusation against the Armenians.** The chronicle recounts tensions at Sebastia, Gagik's intervention, and the political consequences of accusations against the Armenian population. The imported passage is an indexed excerpt.
37. **Section 637 — Baldwin's death and dynastic ties in Cilicia.** The entry records the death of Baldwin, political developments around Prakana, and marriage alliances involving Leon's family. The imported passage is an indexed excerpt.
38. **Section 641 — Famine and the death of Kilij Arslan II.** A severe famine in 1192 is followed by notices about the death of the Seljuk sultan and Saladin's interest in Antioch.
39. **Section 647 — Leon's coronation and the prosperity of Cilicia.** Leon is crowned king of Armenia; the chronicle then praises his generosity and the flourishing of religious and civic life. The available text is an indexed excerpt.
40. **Section 655 — A dispute over the succession in Antioch.** Leon's claim is contested by the new count of Tripoli, leading to a serious dispute with the patriarch of Antioch.
41. **Sections 656–658 — Constantinople, church politics, and succession.** A combined indexed passage records the Latin capture of Constantinople, disputes involving the Armenian Catholicosate, and succession politics in Cilicia. Digilib presents these entries together in one indexed result, so their exact boundaries have not been reconstructed.
42. **Section 660 — Reconciliation and the death of Zakare.** The entry records the death of Catholicos David, a reconciliation between the king and the Catholicos, and the death of the Georgian prince Zakare.
43. **Section 668 — Leon's final counsel and the succession of Zabel.** The chronicle describes the transfer of Hromkla to the Catholicosate and King Leon's final instructions concerning his daughter and successor. The imported passage is an indexed excerpt.
44. **Section 669 — A revolt against the Armenian bailiff.** The available excerpt describes unrest among Armenian and Greek nobles and the response of Constantine, the Armenian bailiff.
45. **Section 708 — Hethum's diplomacy and an ecclesiastical appointment.** Hethum reconciles his son-in-law in Tripoli; the entry also records a church council in Tarsus and the ordination of Baldwin. Only the political and ecclesiastical portion of the indexed text is included.


46. **Section 631 — Ruben's detention in Antioch.** Leon returns from Constantinople to his brother Ruben; political and personal tensions lead to Ruben's detention by the prince of Antioch. The page was opened directly.
47. **Section 642 — Leon and the prince of Antioch; changes in the Catholicosate.** The entry records Leon's dealings with the prince of Antioch, Saladin's demands, and developments in the Armenian Church. The source page combines sections 642–643; this text is split at the explicit dated-entry boundary.
48. **Section 643 — Gregory Apirat and dynastic arrangements.** The continuation records Gregory Apirat's appointment and marriage arrangements linking the Armenian and Antiochene ruling houses. It comes from the combined 642–643 page.
49. **Section 645 — A diplomatic gift from the Byzantine emperor.** The emperor sends Leon a precious crown and seeks a friendly alliance. The page was opened directly.
50. **Section 650 — Tughril Shah and the city of Karin.** The entry describes the succession in Karin and Tughril Shah's friendly relationship with King Leon. The page was opened directly.
51. **Section 652 — The election of Catholicos John.** The death of Gregory and the election of John as Catholicos are recorded. The available text is an indexed excerpt; completeness is unverified.
52. **Section 675 — Hethum's accession.** The Armenian princes and Catholicos Constantine choose Hethum as king and arrange his marriage to Zabel, after which the chronicle notes a period of peace.
53. **Section 705 — Hethum's return and Leon's knighting.** The entry records Hethum's return from the Mongol court, a campaign in the region, and the ceremonial knighting of his eldest son. The page was opened directly.
54. **Section 720 — Leon's coronation and diplomacy.** Leon is crowned king; the entry then records diplomatic exchanges with Abaqa Khan and the birth of a royal son. The available text is an indexed excerpt; completeness is unverified.
55. **Section 721 — Snow in Cilicia and the death of Vardapet Kirakos.** Only the dated notice about snowfall and Kirakos's death is retained. The remainder, which concerns a poisoning plot, is intentionally omitted and has not been reconstructed.
