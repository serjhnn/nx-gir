# Contents

## Smbat Sparapet — *Տարեգիրք* (Chronicle)

This contents guide describes the twenty-two Grabar sections currently imported into this directory. The numbered entries follow the Digilib section identifiers; they are not consecutive chapter numbers. Descriptions are brief reader-oriented summaries, not translations. They avoid reproducing graphic passages.

1. **Section 410 — The Armenian coronation of Gagik.** The entry records Gagik's formal anointing as king in Ani and then turns to a change of ruler in the Byzantine Empire.
2. **Section 418 — Byzantine imperial succession.** A chronicle entry focused on the Byzantine court and the succession of emperors.
3. **Section 421 — Armenia and the neighbouring powers.** Political and military developments involving Armenian rulers and the Byzantine-Arab frontier.
4. **Section 424 — Armenian rule and captivity.** Events surrounding an Armenian ruler, regional power struggles, and captivity.
5. **Section 432 — A new Catholicos and the Georgian-Armenian alliance.** The succession of Catholicos Vahan by Step'annos, followed by a major threat to the region and the cooperation of Armenian and Georgian forces.
6. **Section 494 — Earthquake and political upheaval.** A devastating earthquake in 1045 is followed by accounts of conflicts involving Byzantine, Persian, and regional powers.
7. **Section 508 — Sebastia and the Armenian princes.** The chronicle describes the crisis around Sebastia and the position of Armenian rulers under Byzantine authority.
8. **Section 511 — Raids, compassion, and regional response.** Raids in Armenian districts are recounted alongside the efforts of a local ruler to secure the release of captives.
9. **Section 523 — Gregory's travels and the Armenian Church abroad.** Gregory's journey through Constantinople, Rome, and Egypt, his contacts with Christian communities, and developments in the Armenian Church.
10. **Section 541 — Epidemic, a holy relic, and a change of power.** An epidemic and a dispute over a revered relic appear alongside political developments following the death of Malik-Shah.
11. **Section 546 — The First Crusade and Antioch.** Events connected with the First Crusade and the changing political landscape around Antioch.
12. **Section 547 — The Crusader states and their neighbours.** Further accounts of the Crusader principalities and the political relationships among powers in the region.
13. **Section 562 — Edessa and the displacement of its people.** The entry recounts a crisis in Edessa, the departure and eventual return of its inhabitants, and wider events involving the Crusader rulers.
14. **Section 598 — Cilician Armenia and the changing balance of power.** Events around Marash and Tilbashar, including the capture and negotiated release of an Armenian prince's family.
15. **Section 610 — Ani and Georgian-Armenian regional politics.** Campaigns involving the Georgian king Giorgi and the city of Ani, alongside developments concerning Antioch and neighbouring rulers.
16. **Section 616 — The succession of Catholicos Nerses.** The long service of Catholicos Grigoris, the election of Nerses, and the latter's reputation for learning and sacred poetry.
17. **Section 618 — Mleh's rule in Cilicia.** A strongly critical account of Mleh's seizure of power and oppressive conduct in Cilicia. The source includes passages that are not reproduced in this description.
18. **Section 622 — Gregory Tgha becomes Catholicos.** The death of Catholicos Nerses and the appointment of Gregory Tgha, with attention to his learning and the embellishment of the cathedral.
19. **Section 625 — A treaty between the Seljuk sultan and the Byzantine emperor.** Kilij Arslan II confronts the Byzantine emperor near Melitene, then releases him after an agreement is reached.
20. **Section 626 — The death of Manuel I Komnenos.** The death of the Byzantine emperor Manuel I and the accession of his son Alexios.
21. **Section 717 — Hethum, Abaqa Khan, and Antioch.** A diplomatic mission involving Hethum's court is followed by the events leading to the fall of Antioch in 1268.
22. **Section 718 — Earthquake and diplomacy in Cilicia.** A destructive earthquake in Cilicia is followed by Prince Leon's visit to Abaqa Khan and several deaths in the ruling family.
